Conditional Release: digital escrow that plans for failure

Two parties upload or build an agreement. Excro turns it into a signed Release Schedule that covers what happens when the deal goes well and when it fails. The bank holds the money, and funds move only when verified evidence meets the schedule.

Positioning vs CastlerX

CastlerX already offers rule-based payouts, AI agreement extraction, virtual sub-accounts under a master escrow account, and dispute holds, through 15+ partner banks. Matching that list won't win. Excro has to win on two things CastlerX treats as secondary:

  • The failure path is agreed before any money moves. For each deal, the AI proposes what happens if funding is short, the seller doesn't ship, the goods are lost, the buyer goes silent, the order is only partly accepted, or a deduction applies. Both parties accept these rules up front, so a failed deal settles by rule, and only real disagreements need a person to arbitrate.
  • Evidence is verified, not just uploaded. A dispatch slip is read, cross-checked and confirmed against the courier's own tracking. An invoice is checked against its GST e-invoice record. Uploaded files are only the starting input.
Honest risk: CastlerX can copy both features. Excro's lasting advantage has to be distribution: its existing corporate clients on YES, Axis, IDFC and ICICI who already pay through Excro. Treat the playbook as a head start, not a moat.

Five hard rules

  1. The bank holds the money; Excro sends instructions.Funds sit in a bank escrow account (a virtual account per deal) under a three-way escrow agreement. Excro is the technology provider that sends release instructions, and never holds or pools client funds.
  2. Only the signed Release Schedule moves money.The AI extracts and proposes. Both parties review, edit settings and e-sign. The schedule's hash goes into the signed PDF, which is annexed to the escrow agreement. The bank acts on that schedule, not on the uploaded contract.
  3. Every condition has proof, an owner, a deadline and a timeout action.A condition with no deadline, or with no rule for what happens when it's missed, blocks the schedule from being signed.
  4. Every rupee has a destination in every outcome.Before signing, the platform lists every possible end state (full release, refund, partial release, cancellation, dispute) and checks that payouts plus fees add up exactly to the deposit. An incomplete money map can't be signed.
  5. The AI never decides a payout.Deductions are formulas with caps, agreed up front. At runtime, deterministic code applies the schedule to verified evidence. The AI has no ability to call payouts, so a malicious instruction hidden in an uploaded document can't move money.

Fraud checks

  • Tracking number already used in another Excro deal → blocked and flagged.
  • Courier shows a booking date earlier than the deal's funding date → flagged (possible reused slip).
  • Destination pincode mismatch, or delivered to a different city → delivery not verified; buyer and seller notified; no release.
  • Declared weight far below what the agreement implies (e.g. 40 laptops shipped as 2 kg) → sent for review.

When no courier API is available

For self-delivery, hand delivery, or couriers we haven't integrated: Excro sends the buyer a one-time delivery OTP. The buyer gives it to the seller only at handover, and the seller enters it with a geo-tagged photo. That OTP counts as proof of delivery. If neither is possible, the schedule falls back to the buyer confirming delivery in the app, and both parties see that this is a weaker proof.

What tracking can't prove: "Delivered" shows a parcel with that tracking number arrived. It doesn't show what was inside. That's why the inspection window and the partial-acceptance and return rules below exist. Say this clearly in marketing, or the first "brick in the box" case turns into a complaint about the platform.

Scenario Playbook

After extraction, the AI goes through the failure modes for that deal type and proposes one rule for each. Rules come from a lawyer-approved clause library. Parties can change the numbers (days, %, caps) freely. Changing the wording marks the clause as custom, and it needs Excro's legal review before signing. The AI's job is to spot gaps and set sensible defaults, not to draft law.

Money waterfall & deductions

Every outcome is worked out with the same waterfall. The parties see it for every end state before signing (the mockup shows it working).

LineRule
DepositDeal value plus each payer's share of the Excro commission, when it is collected from escrow
− Excro commissionPer the deal's commission plan (default zero): amount, who pays, and whether it's withdrawn from escrow at funding, at release, or invoiced outside escrow. Withdrawal at funding is always non-refundable (spec 14). The example below uses 0.5%, split 50/50, taken at release.
− Agreed deductionsFormula-based only (late-delivery penalty per day, shortfall at unit price, cancellation fee), each with a cap; go to the party named in the schedule

Bank transfer charges are shown on their own line, charged to whoever the schedule names (default: the recipient).

Disputes

  • Freeze only what's disputed. Undisputed amounts keep moving. Holding the whole deposit hurts the honest party.
  • Stage 1: settle in the platform (7 days). Both parties see the evidence side by side. Either can propose a split; once both e-sign it, it's carried out automatically.
  • Stage 2: online arbitration. Escalates to an online dispute resolution partner (e.g. Presolv360 or SAMA) named in the escrow agreement. The award is uploaded, checked, and carried out as an instruction.
  • Excro never judges. Excro doesn't decide who's right. Taking that role invites liability and regulatory attention.

Phase 1 scope

Sized for a 2-person build team. Anything outside this list waits.

  • One bank: whichever of Excro's four banks approves escrow virtual accounts and payout-by-instruction for a technology provider fastest.
  • One deal type: B2B sale of goods with inspection (covers most of the playbook). Service milestones and joint-venture splits come in Phase 2.
  • Couriers: one aggregator API for coverage, plus the delivery-OTP fallback. Direct courier integrations only once volume justifies them.
  • Disputes: in-platform settlement plus one online arbitration partner, handled manually at first.
  • Channels: web app and WhatsApp notifications. Parties act in the web app; WhatsApp nudges them.
